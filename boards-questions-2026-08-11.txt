HPI: which of the following medications induces and increases the amplitude of colonic high-amplitude propagated contractions?
Workup & course: 
Prompt: Which option is the best?
Possible Answers:
  A. lubiprostone
  B. plecanatide
  C. prucalopride
  D. linaclotide
My selected Answer: I'm not sure
Rationale: No idea, I'm afrad

What answer do you think is best and why?

Answer is C. prucalopride

Key concept
High‑amplitude propagated contractions (HAPCs) are the large, sweeping colonic contractions responsible for mass movement and defecation.

Only one class of drugs is known to induce and increase the amplitude of HAPCs:

5‑HT4 receptor agonists → prucalopride
Prucalopride directly stimulates enteric neurons to generate coordinated, powerful colonic motility waves.

This is why it is used for chronic idiopathic constipation and why it improves colonic transit time.

Why the other options are wrong
A. Lubiprostone
* Chloride channel activator
* Increases intestinal fluid secretion
* Improves stool consistency
* Does NOT increase HAPCs

B. Plecanatide
* Guanylate cyclase‑C agonist
* Increases chloride/bicarbonate secretion
* Helps stool hydration
* Does NOT induce HAPCs

D. Linaclotide
* Also a GC‑C agonist
* Same mechanism as plecanatide
* Does NOT increase HAPCs
* These drugs improve constipation by increasing secretion, not by generating mass‑movement contractions.
